Why your store may be invisible to AI assistants

A practical test you can run on your own storefront in about a minute, and what to do about the result.

Here is a test worth a minute of your time. Open your own store in a browser with JavaScript switched off. Whatever is still on the screen is roughly what a machine sees when it fetches your page. For a lot of modern storefronts, what is left is a logo and a spinner.

Three questions, in order

Whether an assistant can recommend your store comes down to three things, and they happen in sequence. Failing the first makes the other two irrelevant.

Can it be fetched?

The crawlers behind the major assistants request your page and read what comes back in that first response. They do not wait for a framework to boot, they do not click, they do not log in, and they do not run the script that fills your product grid. If your catalog is drawn client-side, there is nothing in that response to read.

Can it be parsed?

A price is only a price if it is characters in the HTML. Rendered into an image, injected by a script, or shown only after a variant is selected, it cannot be quoted. A machine that cannot state your price will quote a competitor who publishes theirs plainly. The same goes for sizes, materials, shipping and returns.

Can it be trusted as current?

The ugliest failure in machine-generated recommendations is not an invented store. It is a real store with a dead price or a product that sold out eighteen months ago. Undated records get treated as risky, and risky records get skipped. A visible verification date is cheap to publish and does more work than almost anything else on the page.

What actually fixes it

Server-render the facts. Write prices, dimensions and policies as text in the initial response, and mark them up in schema.org as well, because the markup is what makes you eligible for product feed programs. Publish a catalog feed at a stable URL that does not move. Put a date on the record and keep it current.

None of that is exotic, and none of it requires replatforming. It does require someone to do it and keep doing it, which is the part most independent stores never get to.

What we will not claim

Nobody can promise you a citation in an assistant, a search ranking, or a sale, and you should be suspicious of anyone who does — those systems are not ours to control. What can be promised is that every fact about your store is published in a form a machine can fetch, parse and date. That is the part that is currently missing, and it is the part we do.
