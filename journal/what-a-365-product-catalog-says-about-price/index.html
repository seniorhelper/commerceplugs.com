What a 365-product accessible-bathing catalog says about price

We indexed every published product from one independent retailer and looked at what the numbers actually show about how accessible bathing is priced.

Most writing about bathroom remodelling quotes a single average and moves on. An average is the least useful number you can publish about a catalog like this one, because the catalog is not one product. It is a shower seat at a hundred and eighty dollars and a steam enclosure at nine thousand, sold by the same shop to the same customer on the same afternoon.

What we counted

Showers4Less, an independent retailer in Denver operated by Aging Safely Baths, publishes a public catalog. We read it in full on the date below, kept every item that carries a real published price, and classified each by the type the seller themselves assigns it. Nothing here is estimated or modelled. Where the seller publishes nothing, we publish nothing.

The spread is the finding

Price in this category does not cluster. It separates into bands that correspond to how much building work the product implies. Parts and fittings sit lowest, because they drop into an opening that already exists. Pans and doors sit in the middle, because they replace a component without moving plumbing. Complete systems and steam sit highest, because buying one is a decision about the room rather than about the fixture.

That matters for a shopper because the band tells you what question you are actually answering. If you are looking at seats and grab bars you are solving a safety problem this month. If you are looking at roll-in systems you are rebuilding a room, and the price of the unit is a fraction of what the project costs.

Brand concentration

One other thing the data shows plainly: an independent specialist is not carrying forty brands. It carries a handful, deeply. That is the opposite of how a marketplace listing page looks, and it is most of why a specialist is worth asking.

Why we publish this

Because it is checkable. Every figure above is derived from records published on this site, each with a verification date, and the underlying data is available as an open feed. If the catalog changes, these numbers change with it, and the date on the page tells you which catalog you are reading about.
